# Vocabulary bank — candidates

Candidates for hover definitions. Pick the ones you want; everything here is a suggestion, not a decision.

## How these were picked

There is no standard "use count" that makes a word a glossary term. The closest established tools:

- **Keyness** (corpus linguistics): compare how often a word appears in your text vs. general English. A log-likelihood score ≥ 15.13 means p < 0.0001 that the overuse is chance ([UCREL LL calculator](https://ucrel.lancs.ac.uk/llwizard.html); [Gabrielatos, *Keyness Analysis*](https://research.edgehill.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/20078913/Gabrielatos.Keyness.postprint.pdf)). This filters out ordinary words like "the" or "and".
- **Term extraction** ([Frantzi et al., C-value method](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s007999900023)): rank multi-word phrases by frequency, and don't count a short phrase separately when it mostly appears inside a longer one ("cognitive light cone" vs "light cone"). Frequency cutoffs in this literature are set per text, not fixed.
- **Wikipedia's rule** ([MOS:Linking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Linking)): link by *need*, not count — "Link a term at most once per major section, at first occurrence."

**Rule used here** (57,000 words, 33 files: Introduction + all sections):

1. Strongly overused vs. general English (log-likelihood ≥ 15.13 and at least 4× more frequent).
2. **Core** = used **≥ 10 times in ≥ 3 sections**, meaning a reader meets it away from where it's defined, which is when a hover helps.
3. Your coined or defined terms are listed separately even below 10 uses, since a reader can't know them without a definition.
4. Then hand-filtered to concepts only; everyday words that are just frequent are listed at the end.

Counts include plurals and close forms (e.g. "chunk / chunks / chunked / chunking"). "First" is the section where the term first appears, usually where you'd write the definition.

## Things to know when you pick

- **Overlaps:** "contraction" vs "haptic contraction" vs "latched contraction", "prior" vs "deep prior", "layer" vs the three named layers, "inference" vs "active inference". You may want one entry with sub-definitions rather than separate ones.
- **Two senses:** "transparency" first appears in 1.2 as *phenomenal transparency* (the field can't itself be seen); in 6.1/6.2 it's the practice of seeing a gestalt as constructed. One hover definition would need to cover both, or you could use two. "field" sometimes means "area of study".
- **Order matters:** several terms are used before they're defined. For example, "friction" and "resonance" first appear in 2.1 but are defined in 3.4. Hover definitions would help most there.
