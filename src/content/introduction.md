title: Introduction
---
Humanity's story, or rather that of a “universal” story, is characterized by increasing specificity. As things march along the ever-so-progressive process of entropic evolution, what naturally arises is a sense of increase in complexity. More and more things start to connect and depend on each other, creating systems that have feedback loops and downstream effects, eventually becoming incredibly chaotic. But through this process is a demarcation of specificity, the act of things becoming more pointy.&#x20;

The human story very obviously outlines this: we’ve gone from primitive living in caves to developing and living in chaotic cities with vast interconnected economic systems and advanced architecture, something a human from even a few thousand years ago could literally never imagine. It is clear the action and adjective of creativity is of upmost importance here, as without it, the human species would be doomed to a state of static repetition, something seen in even our closest relatives, chimpanzees, [who cannot innovate tools across generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_effect#Cultural_anthropology). But what makes human specificity special? Specifically, why does our biology and downstream phenomenology allow for such impactful things?&#x20;

I want to try to answer this in my own terms using my own reasoning, working with years of experience studying human perception, psychology, and behavior as an amateur. In this piece, I attempt to sketch out a working ontology explaining what we are doing in perception that is so important, and how this affects what we do and the systems we participate in. I do posit some riskable predictions that have their own consequences, but most of this work is a synthesis of past work done by many others. After all, this is the human story right? Drawing on current knowledge and taking steps to go into a specific direction is one way we are special as a species. I do not claim to have solved anything, or that my work is 100% correct in all areas, or I am an expert so you should take my word for it, but I do think my generative reframing of certain problems is valuable and has practical implications.

> “There is indeed the inexpressible. This shows itself; it is the mystical.”
>
> — Ludwig Wittgenstein, Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, 6.522

This one proposition by Wittgenstein shows us the ineffable. Some things, ethics, aesthetics, love, are only fully comprehensible when not put to language. For Wittgenstein, the mystical here is the boundary where language breaks down and cannot express what the one who speaks is attempting to convey. There remains a mystery, a sort of unexplainable frustration that the most important things, the things we dedicate our lives to, cannot be expressed without losing their immense meaning. This problem of the inexpressible remains a frustration we have to deal with. As long as we have to communicate in a finite, compressible manner, we will never be able to share what hangs in our bodies. Fortunately, not all is lost.

Language here is not a complete failure as it allows us to transport seemingly fleeting experiences into symbols that others can understand themselves. However, it fails in the sense that the fleeting experience is never fully captured because to turn it into tokens of interpretable symbols, we must use compression. Compression in this framework means preserving contextually important structure while leaving other characteristics out. As Wittgenstein put it, the inexpressible can only show itself, not tell itself. Lots of experience then is ineffable since a compressed expression that attempts to capture it cannot preserve the full richness of what it proceeds.

Knowledge (which I am defining here as information encoded in a form that can shape perception) shows up in the world through many forms such as a book, a television show, a conversation, or a diagram. But these are all expressions of something deeper. Below the obvious surface of notation are two more layers, each so dense with information that it is puzzling why they stay hidden. The paradox of these layers being hidden resolves in a single mechanism known as compression. Compression of knowledge loses richness but gains portability, stability, and shareability. To externalize deeper layers beyond surface level notation is to compress them, and most of what we know, what we feel, cannot be compressed in totality.
