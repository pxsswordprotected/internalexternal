title: Introduction
---
As things march along the process of entropic evolution, what naturally arises is a sense of increase in complexity. More and more things start to connect and depend on each other, creating systems that have feedback loops and downstream effects, eventually becoming chaotic. But through this process is a demarcation of specificity, the act of things becoming more pointy.

The human story very obviously outlines this: we’ve gone from primitive living in caves to developing and living in chaotic cities with vast interconnected economic systems and advanced architecture. It is clear creativity is of upmost importance here, as without it, the human species would be doomed to a state of static repetition, something seen in even our closest relatives, chimpanzees, [who cannot innovate tools across generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratchet_effect#Cultural_anthropology). The specific question I am interested in is why does our mind-body system and phenomenology allow for creation of such grandiose things? And if we can find that out, what other questions and answers does that lead to?

This piece is my attempt to answer this,  working with years of experience studying human perception, psychology, and behavior as an amateur. Here, I attempt to sketch out a working ontology explaining what we are doing in perception that is so important, and how this affects what we do and the systems we participate in. I do not claim to have solved anything, or that my work is 100% correct in all areas, or I am an expert so you should take my word for it, but I do think my reframing of certain problems is generative and valuable, which has practical implications. Think of this piece as a way for me to explain what I think about certain problems and how they fit within the framework.&#x20;

As a whole, this piece is about exploring a unified set of principles that explain how perception, memory, emotion, and much more follow from the same underlying processes. This matters because the way we design things such as schools, tools, media, technology, medicine, and whole societies depends on models of how people work. If the model we are using when building these things is wrong, we build them in such a way to work against us. If we can deeply understand the rules that shape subjective experience, which in turn shapes "objective" experience like scientific discovery, we can design for human flourishing in a much more intentional way. We ourselves can also use these principles in a way that reduces our own suffering and allows for us to increase our own well-being.&#x20;

This document is also always in the process of being updated. As my own beliefs and ontology change, so does this piece. This does not mean the current information presented is wrong or outdated, it simply means we always have more work to do on the forefront of discovery and to update towards new information is an act of courage.&#x20;
