The previous section described failed higher order gestalts through lower level priors not being stable enough for the whole to appear. Creativity can be understood as a search process that sometimes resolves this failure. It keeps different chunks active until one becomes sufficiently supported to register as a new gestalt, something novel. Structural thresholds explain why this search can develop gradually while its result feels like a sudden movement from “here” to “there.” A structural threshold is the critical point where enough lower-level chunks become sufficiently stable to abruptly percolate into an available higher-order gestalt.





I’ve long since been fascinated by this tweet by  because it displays something very useful: the way that moving from “here” to “there” so often feels like a discontinuous jump with no clear progressive path between the two, like his example of making money online being totally unthinkable until you do it, and then totally obvious everyone’s doing it. The discontinous jump part here in particular makes sense in the light of this framework and how it views creativity. Creativity here is a search process through a possibility space, with a target of stabilizing existing chunks into seemingly novel gestalt(s). You can only operate from the chunks your input history and field has learned, so every new idea is a novel manipulation of existing knowledge the field actively uses to search for answers to targets. 

As established in the training data section, the chunks and relations available to the field constrain where creative search can begin and occur. Most people would be better advised to diversify their input in order to train more rich and varied chunks for more combinatorial power. Importantly, this means there is no absolute novelty, since there are only combinations that are new relative to a given individual field, hence why one idea can feel like an obvious next step to someone and a giant leap in innovation to another. The person who made up the novel idea has a field that already stored the needed chunks and the paths between them, so the new combination sits close on their prediction tree and is reachable. To an individual who lacks those needed chunks, the idea isn’t a nearby search at all since the prerequisites aren’t stored. The felt “out-there-ness” of an idea is not an inherent property of the thing but a measurement of the distance between the field that produced it and the field encountering it. This is what we can call creative distance: the amount of reorganization required for a new gestalt to become reachable from the creator’s current field. 

Lesswrong has modeled something related called ‘inferential distance,’ which is the “amount of steps or concepts a person needs to share before they can successfully communicate the object level point.” This differs from creative distance because it is spelling out the difference in prerequisite structure to result in perception rather than creation. 

This also emphasizes the importance of knowledge graphs and mapping out prerequisites, since certain chunks are literally needed before learning and using ideas that sit higher on the tree of knowledge. It’s like the load-bearing tree: some structure of the trunk is needed before branching out. 

As a caveat, recombination is not the only source of novelty in the field. Open loops have the ability to import constraints the field could not have generated internally due to lacking prerequisites or deep attractor basins which prevent the correct structure from being reachable. That is why doing the work and practicing produces understanding that just “thinking harder” does not, and why a closed loop can refine structure indefinitely without adding anything novel. So “no absolute novelty” means newness is relative to a given field, not that nothing new is imported. In terms of a starting point within the human system (and other agentic systems), this framework does not attempt to map out where structure originates in the first place, or why any specific pattern shows up in a system at all, which is what people like Michael Levin are attempting to do. This framework’s intentional boundary takes it that our starting point comes from “innate” structure like interoceptive homeostasis, genetics, and evolutionary pressures. 

This is ultimately what the tweet is pointing at. Moving from “here” to “there” follows the same general kind of dynamic as gestalt formation, only across a longer time scale. Before the transition, partial prerequisites and candidate organizations may be accumulating without any alternative becoming stable enough to appear. Once enough supporting structure has developed, a thing crosses a threshold and becomes available as a new higher order gestalt. The experienced change can therefore feel discontinuous even though the structural changes supporting it were gradual. The change is not binary at the structural level because what appears abrupt is the resulting change in what the system can perceive.

From the field of the “before you” that was attempting to get into the state of the new thing, the new thing maybe be unavailable to access because the necessary priors have not been learned, or because existing priors cannot yet be activated and coordinated into the needed organization. After the threshold is crossed, the new gestalt reorganizes what seems obvious, making it difficult to reconstruct why “there” was once so hard to see from “here.”

Importantly, once a new gestalt has crossed the threshold and been used repeatedly through practice, its organization can itself become a reusable chunk. Meaning, the gestalt can form relationships with other gestalts which then form a structure by which input is organized. What was previously the result of creative search then becomes one of the things available for future search. It’s like learning a new math skill that then becomes useable for learning other skills. Creativity therefore expands its own possibility space over time since a current emergent gestalt can become part of the structure supporting a future higher order gestalt.

This is the underlying mechanism Kenneth Stanley and Joel Lehman were getting at with their book ‘Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned.’ Planning an end goal requires the destination to be a branch you can already see and take. The priors and paths that would allow you to see the true end goal at the beginning are built by the journey itself, so the start is simply that: a naive way of beginning a journey that is needed to loop for refining knowledge and action. At the start, the endpoint is genuinely outside your representable space. You reach the “there” by crossing a threshold that allows you to see a new gestalt that was once unimaginable, and you can no longer quite say why it was ever hard to see from “here.”  

These core observations about novelty can help us understand the importance of constraints when creating. Moderate, well-chosen constraints boost creativity, too few cause paralysis with infinite possibility, and too many shut the system down due to not having enough slack. This parallels the edge-of-the-field zone that underlies learning and positive differences: too few constraints leaves the search space impossibly large, so there is no path cheap enough to resolve into a gestalt (paralysis), while too many minimizes the space so much that nothing can be combined into something novel; the sweet spot is where constraints minimize the space to something navigable while leaving enough slack to recombine chunks into a gestalt that wasn’t there before. There is an underlying point here that creativity is search through a possibility space within an ontological framing and the search needs structure to be feasible. Constraints prune certain branches of the load-bearing tree so enough precision can be used for action so a new combination is interpreted as meaningful. 

We can take something like play being helpful for creativity since play is accepting constraints that structure the search space of a situation while leaving enough slack for exploration of meaningful recombinations. A frame of play also enables us to place less precision on branches that would constrain our search too much, like needed validation. If we are in a state of play, we can “override” what we would normally value which can result in creative acts we otherwise would not stumble on. Play is valuable because it lowers the predicted cost of failed output without removing structure from the creative search entirely.

In essence, training data gives us available structures that can be used and organized around, the field’s propertiesm like constraintsm organize the search space, slack and play keep alternative branches in the load-bearing tree alive, and the Loop of Refinement generates and tests organizations of things. Crucially, a structural threshold is crossed when certain input becomes sufficiently stable to appear as a new gestalt, which is how we get from “here” to “there” in terms of state. As we move to novel states and gestalts, the field can represent its new organization which results in more action policies that are made available through branches of the load-bearing tree.
