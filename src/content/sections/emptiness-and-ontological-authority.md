Emptiness, or suññatā, is an extremely load bearing concept within this framework that leads to insight about time, wonder, suffering, and frankly everything. As a primer, here is what Rob Burbea has to say about emptiness: “We can, at least for now, define emptiness as the absence of this inherent existence that things appear to naturally and undeniably have” and “Emptiness then, is more a quality that we can recognize in something, a way something is, a property it has. A thing is ‘empty’ of its seemingly real, independent existence.” Emptiness means that no “thing” exists in the independent manner it appears to. Its identity and properties depend on causes, conditions, relations, and the field organizing them into a specific gestalt. I like to think of emptiness as seeing through an optical illusion and realizing that multiple interpretations can be valid as perceptual organizations, while none exist inherently within the drawings/lines themselves. The conditions and manner in which you look at the illusion determine what appears. It is empty of being one way or another. It is flexible, just like all other “things.” Emptiness can be a simple tool, like understanding that your perspective depends on your conditioning and mood, but taken to great depths, it is an amazing tool for addressing problems we think we have, like suffering.

To fit emptiness into this framework, it is important to know that it is the negative space of the model, not another component among the others, but a way in which the core components are seen from the other side, like the back of a coin compared to the front. Every component and mechanism so far, like compression, chunking, the field, gestalts, and the load-bearing tree, has been modeled to understand how “things” get constructed. Emptiness is simply telling us that the construction of “things” is just that: a construction. The constructive process runs roughly as follows:

priors structure sensory experience into features and relations → those relations are compressed into chunks → chunks are packaged as a gestalt → precision makes the gestalt “stable” and useable

Emptiness actually reverses this process:

the solid thing is examined as a gestalt → gestalt is de-chunked into its relation and parts → relations are traced back to the causes and conditions allowing it to appear

To see a thing as empty is to understand how the field is constructing and maintaining it. For instance, a chair is an object gestalt produced from a field configuration, built from co-arising conditions like shapes, relations, context, and prior knowledge. If you change the priors or their weighting, the “thing” of the chair appears differently or not at all, which is exactly what happens with the duck rabbit illusion and the unreadable image. Emptiness is not nihilism or a denial of realness since the chair remains real and useful at its level from a physical point of view, but it is not “real” as it appears in experience independently of the processes that make it appear and function as what is registered as chair.

Gestalt appearances do not require a thing to receive complete ontological authority. The system needs enough compression and precision for a chair to appear and function as a chair, but it does not need to treat “chair” as an inherent structure that exists independently of the field. Ontological authority in this framework is the degree to which the field builds on top of a gestalt once it has emerged, like how many priors depend on it. To be clear about the difference between precision weighting and ontological authority, we can say precision is about how strongly the system holds a prediction, not about dependent structures. Crucially, you can lower a gestalt's authority while it still appears clearly in experience. For instance, "dog" can appear as a real thing, while very little has to be built on top like "all dogs are dangerous" which can produce subsequent action like clenching muscles. The gestalt is fully available, but the field has stopped automatically building predictions and actions on top of it.&nbsp;

If “I am unsafe” has high ontological authority, the field still makes predictions about input, but ambiguous input is assimilated into branches associated with “I am unsafe” and lowers the precision assigned to safety evidence. An unsafe attractor state may therefore have a deep or wide basin due to high ontological authority, making alternative configurations difficult to reach. In the attractor state, only certain elements like specific priors have high ontological authority, not necessarily everything within the field configuration. However, high ontological authority does not necessarily always give way to a deep or wide basin. A thing can be authoritative within a narrow context yet disappear outside it, like a certain frame applied by choice. As a more flexible example with lower authority, a chair can appear clearly and guide the act of sitting without its “chairness” governing every interpretation. It can become a step, sculpture, or pile of wood, so its ontological authority is contextual and limited. Ontological authority differs from reification because reification concerns a construction appearing independently as “real” so it is more static, while ontological authority concerns how strongly that construction governs further perception and action. Reification is about how the gestalt appears to the system, while authority is about how the system uses it. This also connects to the process of real-making as laid out in the loop of refinement section as anything that is made real through the loop, such as religion, has high(er) ontological authority.

Ontological authority determines how much of the field a violation has to propagate through, which is what the energetic triad measures as cost. High authority means many other priors rest on the gestalt, so revising it forces recomputation of everything built on top. A low authority gestalt can be dropped cheaply because little structure was resting on it. This helps us refine the definition of what depth implies in the load-bearing tree: a prior’s depth matches the degree to which other gestalts use it as a premise, and updating cost comes from partly how many of the dependent priors must be recomputed. This also means authority is self-maintaining because a high-authority gestalt structures ambiguous input, so that input integrates cheaply in the field and reads as confirming evidence for the active predictions while counter-input is encoded as friction to be explained away. This means resonance and friction end up reinforcing whatever already has authority rather than telling you whether it is accurate. This is why open refinement loops are important so we do not fall into the trap of treating cheap integration of input as evidence that we are right. This also means the two orientations the refinement loops take, real-making and truth-making matter: real-making raises ontological authority which makes real-made things expensive to update, and truth-making requires exposing it to external feedback the system cannot plan for. Authority also keeps integration of input cheap by pruning the search space of the load-bearing tree’s branches, so lowering it means holding open alternatives the system had stopped computing, which is why lowering authority requires slack and tends to fail when the system has little.

Connecting this to somatics, it seems as though rigid or latched contraction is ontological authority held in the body. This is why a simple representation of safety typically doesn't work against a highly tense body. Haptic contraction gives a gestalt authority in the embodied substrate, since the contraction a prediction produces becomes interoceptive input that the predicted state is real, and interoceptive priors sit deep in the load-bearing tree so the signals are weighted heavily over most other input. Bringing slack into this, having alternative branches of input available means not latching or contracting, so there is a beginning point of using a lot of resources in order to hold hold other branches possible instead of defaulting to what the field is conditioned towards. A latch, meaning a contraction pattern held long enough to become rigid, does the same job as a premise because holding it in the body keeps one interpretation of input “sitcky” and the system can build on top of it without recomputing the held knowledge.

In terms of the compression gradient, to examine a gestalt for learning or updating, we must first objectify it by making it stable and clear enough to inspect, which means compressing it from the embodied layer, what the load-bearing tree uses as its physical substrate, into a representation or a piece of notation. A gestalt that holds high ontological authority resists compression because it exists as the structure itself doing the compression rather than as an object obvious to be compressed. This is why the inverse of the constructive process is hard: the field has to make stable and inspect what it is currently using as its guiding structure. For instance, a chair decompresses easily because not much else rests on the “chair” construction, while the sense of self does not because almost everything else is built on top of the “sense of self” structure. As another instance, the grammar rules of a language of a native speaker stay hidden because those rules are the structure generating the judgment rather than content available. To describe the highly authoritative grammar rules explicitly requires the hidden structure to be objectified, which means moving from the embodied layer into notation which is essentially the loop of refinement.

To sum up the relationships going on here: precision is how much weight or confidence a prediction is given in the current field. Ontological authority is how strongly an emergent gestalt functions as a structure for organizing further perception and action. Depth describes how many other structures depend on it across contexts, while reification describes the construction becoming invisible and appearing inherent or independently real.

precision makes the gestalt stable and useable → other inference starts treating it as structure, which is authority → dependence accumulates across contexts, which is depth → the construction stops being noticed at all, which is reification.

If we question what is or was high ontological authority, we have the chance to break through our current way of seeing things which is typically the core target of novel discoveries. This is exactly what Grothendieck meant here in this quote:

But let us return to the dream, and to the prohibition that has been imposed on mathematics for thousands of years. This is perhaps the most inveterate of all the preconceptions, often implicit and rooted in habit, which decrees that such and such a thing is “maths” and such other things not. It took millenniums before things as childish and ubiquitous as the groups of symmetries of certain geometric figures, the topological forms of some others, the number zero and the sets found admission into the sanctuary! When I talk to students about the topology of a sphere, and the shapes that can be deduced from a sphere by adding handles - things that don’t surprise young children, but which confuse the students because they think they know what “maths” is - the first spontaneous echo I get is: but this is not maths! Maths, of course, means the Pythagorean theorem, the heights of a triangle and second degree polynomials... These students are no more stupid than you or me, they react as all the mathematicians in the world have reacted from time immemorial until today, except for people like Pythagoras or Riemann and maybe five or six others. Even Poincaré, who was not the first to come along, managed to prove by a well-felt philosophical A plus B that infinite sets were not maths! Surely there must have been a time when triangles and squares were not maths - they were drawings that children or pottery craftsmen traced on the sand or in the clay of vases, so as not to make mistakes...

To be clear, Grothendieck is speaking moreso of categories and frames here like, “When I talk to students about the topology of a sphere…” but categories can just as easily be gestalts as an item like a chair can be. The core difference is in legibility: categories are not typically well defined in the notational sense while items, like chairs, are obviously “there.” Again this is because in the structure the thing is difficult to see clearly: categories and frames are operating as the structure itself which makes it harder to objectify than an object “out there” like a chair.

Emptiness as a tool lowers ontological authority not by preventing gestalts from appearing, but by revealing that their authority is made from causes and conditions, which are mutable. We can use emptiness wisely to develop a more fluid state of being in which we hold more capacity for moving between states. This reduces suffering and increases the potential for intelligence by widening the range of field configurations that remain reachable. Because objects are pointers towards field configurations (section 5.3), seeing an object as empty makes the configuration it was pointing towards&nbsp;directly addressable. This means you can satisfy the lasting properties of the field through other routes than just the ones organized by the original object, or see that the target was associated with deep priors like permanence or selfhood and release the pressure there.&nbsp;

Constructional transparency, a property of a field configuration in relation to particular gestalts, is the practical capacity to maintain this recognition while keeping structuring gestalts alive, allowing them to guide experience without becoming a rigid structure that everything else has to be recomputed against. To be alive, to survive as a human, we must structure sensory input somehow, so some things need high ontological authority. The key question is not “should we stop giving authority to things in general” but rather, "what should we give authority to, how much to give, and in what contexts."
