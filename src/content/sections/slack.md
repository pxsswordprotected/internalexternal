Up to this point slack has been used multiple times without a useful definition: in section 1.2 deep priors needed "sufficient slack" to update and the energetic triad used capacity as a way to measure spent resources before we process input. The quantity and measurement of slack now needs to be explored before going further.

Put simply, slack is the portion of the system's capacity not currently committed to holding its active field configuration in place. Capacity here meaning the system's practical ability to process experience in a given moment, governed through the channels of attention, regulation, representations, physiology, and time. Slack allows competing branches of the dependency tree to be active at the same time instead of closing down on one, which means the system can hold errors as meaningful rather than immediately resolving them through habitual active inference.

Slack is a property of the field, just like felt tone, fit distance, and reachability. It is not a [fuel tank that simply depletes with use](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000083). ["Mental energy runs out" (ego depletion)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873) is not a reliable measurement since it has failed to replicate in the literature. Slack is also not simply metabolic budget, or the ability to "think harder." [Your brain is already consuming most of its required energy simply by being awake and functioning, so focused thought only adds a small amount of cost](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4387513/). [When mental effort feels too costly to the system, the issue is that the brain decides effort is not worth allocating within the given context](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3856320/). [When the system seems to run "out of fuel", the brain has changed how worthwhile an action feels](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3718823/). Factors like [pain](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59410) and [fatigue](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psychsport.2017.08.013) can make the same action feel much harder, causing avoidance or choosing an easier habit to enact. Slack is also not calmness, since [a person can appear calm because they have collapsed their options](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4495877/).

Phenomenologically, because slack is a field property and not something external, it is only visible under disturbance. Just as you cannot see through the field itself, you cannot read your own slack from the inside. For instance, how long you take to recover from a disturbance can be a proxy for the amount of slack the system holds in relation to that context, rather than simply staying calm.

Slack is relational, meaning slack is always slack for a specific thing. Remember, it is not simply a fuel tank that holds universally across all contexts, it is in relation to the task at hand. A person may handle intellectual uncertainty well but have little capacity for social rejection. Slack is useless without bearing in mind the demand at hand. This mirrors physiological reserve since reserve is the "[difference between the basal level of a system and its maximal capacity to respond](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5861884/)." Essentially, it is the gap between what your body is currently doing and the maximum extra demand it can withstand before it stops recovering normally. This also makes environment important, since environment can raise slack by lowering the cost of holding a field configuration, like being with a trusted person. Furthermore, just as slack is relational and responds to certain patterns, a contracted latch in the body is slack permanently used somewhere. It is capacity spent holding one interpretation as fixed, which means less available action and further updating. So releasing a deep latch in the body it not adding something new to experience, but releasing a taxing component that gives more room to the system.

When it comes to environment, predictability matters since it reduces how much spare capacity you need to operate. If actions and interpretations arrive predictability, like having a daily routine, you can operate close to your slack limit without being overwhelmed. Routines and rituals therefore function like slack by reducing surprise the system would have to compute for. Chaotic environments that are unpredictable requires more unused slack to handle the same task. The same person would need more slack in a volatile context to do the same updating as one within a predictable context.

Slack is important because a system with little to no slack acts out existing habits and priors. With no capacity to hold error or competing branches in the dependency tree, the system will revert to a deep configuration and increase precision on existing models rather than exploring and considering alternatives. This implies that low slack simplifies experience before it fragments it. With low slack, the field will collapse competing distinctions it would otherwise hold, first dropping the ones least relevant to a current dominant target. This is why a depleted person’s world goes flat and simplified rather than chaotic. In section 1.3, an asymmetry was noted that collapsing many-to-one is easier than expanding one-to-many, this is because slack is needed to expand. Low slack also narrows diversity of interpretations and options rather than completely closing them in general. A system under threat with low slack is not simply shut off from doing anything, the system will simply update fast towards things that it is already oriented towards. Other options become unavailable, the only thing that matters is going towards the already known thing.

Again, slack is not just one simple number, it is context dependent. Sleep loss can wreck sustained attention but keep up reasoning, and stress can impair flexibility while improving inhibition. There is no "how much slack do I have" number. Instead we might measure it across different dimensions that can interact with each other, like physiology, attentional and working-memory, expected controllability, availability of alternative configurations, environmental and social support, and time to consolidate. Interestingly, some dimensions can "substitute" for one another, like external notation replacing working memory. Because of this, these different dimensions of slack open up different phases of change. At one point you might need enough capacity for attentional mechanisms to work in a certain way to create change, while at another point you might need enough time and safety so a new pattern can stabilize. Simply saying we need "more slack" is too vague, what matters is the interaction type between old and new patterns.

Slack is bound by reachability. It only matters if you can access it from your current state. You might be intelligent and flexible while working, but these abilities can become unreachable when stressed or anxious. The capacity itself exists across certain configurations (since slack is a property), but it is bound to certain causes and conditions and cannot always be accessed in patterns where you are stuck. Capacity that is not held within the configuration you need is not that helpful.

When it comes to slack, having several different ways to achieve the same field configuration is more robust and helpful than having several copies of one path. For instance, three breathing techniques are mostly redundant because anxiety could impair all three, while diverse paths like breaking, walking, and social contact utilize different systems, so there is more of a chance one will work. Diverse paths protect you against problems you might not anticipate, while similar paths tend to fail centering around the same reason. If there is a novel disturbance in the system you have not planned for, one reliable path may be "blocked" and capacity will be withheld.

Slack can become needlessly spent if not kept in line. The mind tends to use any spare capacity to keep predicting and solving for uncertainty, so when "extra" openness appears, the system may spend it by collapsing on another interpretation or action instead of holding it open. Equanimity, as discussed later, has a job here: it lets several possibilties remian open without collapse or a rush to resolve them. This allows slack to exist to be spent wisely instead of being instantly consumed by the system's optimization function.
