In this loop, there exists two main orientations that it can take, which I am calling real-making and truth-making. Just as the open and closed versions of the loop can be ran at parallel, these two orientations of either version can be ran simultaneously to reinforce each other.

Real-making, in general, is the process by which something becomes embodied and actionable within the field. Repetition, attention, aesthetics, emotion, social reinforcement, and practice can all deepen its priors and make it a more reachable field configuration from a global point-of-view. It is making a thing more “real” in the sense of it mattering more to the system. To real-make something is to make it meaningful within a present configuration of the field. This process is moreso correlated with closed loops because what is being repeatedly tested is simply internal with no meaningful external feedback. Of course it is possible we can detect logical errors and misonerms in our models, but this is much harder to do closed because it is relying on your current field configuration to supply you with the necessary working properties.

Resonance helps push real-making further because the feeling can make a representation easier to embody and take seriously as a concept rather than simply discarding it. When a thing is resonant, there’s like a hook attached to it that is hard to let go of, this of course can make things seem a lot more meaningful than without resonance. However, other orthogonal feelings can real-make a thing to like fear and authority so it should be made clear resonance is not necessarily needed for the process. Real-making is about integration and stabilization, not necessarily positive felt tone, so any mechanism that contribute to those two multipliers is what matters here.

Truth-making on the other hand is the process of testing and updating a model so that it better corresponds with reality. The process depends on constraints, like evidence, argument, and other people, that push back against the current field so naturally the process is closely correlated with open loops. The process of truth-making is essential to making things reliable so external feedback gives consistent and useful results. This dynamic shifts when viewed across different levels of abstraction. For example, someone may be “truth-making” within a frame of religion, like debating about ancient texts, but from a higher-order outside perspective of a non-believer they are simply real-making false claims. What the outsider does not understand is that the debaters are indeed truth-making within the ontological constraints the frame they are operating in, and because we are always operating in some type of environment that places structure onto what is possible, all truth-making is ultimately relative to the boundaries and rules set by that environment. Of course resonance is not required for truth-making since accurate information may initially produce friction because it conflicts with existing priors.

To be clear, the loop of refinement itself is a neutral process; it contains no inherent orientation. The loop does not move towards truth on its own, it just modulates whatever its targets and feedback environment select for. An echo chamber contains genuine refinement loops, but they may refine a belief for a target that is not in favor of external correspondence, such as social acceptance or emotional resonance.

Real-making can be useful before truth-making because an idea may be initially be too vague or fragile to test. If we “incubate” the idea long enough through real-making, it can allow us to stabilize and scrutinize the idea instead of us instantly discarding it when we encounter friction. Michael Nielson speaks of something similar by using the concept of “creative cocoons”: “To be creative, you need to recognize those barely formed thoughts, thoughts which are usually wrong and poorly formed in many ways, but which have some kernel of originality and importance and
truth. And if they seem important enough to be worth pursuing, you construct a creative cocoon around them, a set of stories you tell yourself to protect the idea not just from others, but from your own self doubts. The purpose of those stories isn’t to be an air tight defence. It’s to give you the confidence to nurture the idea, possibly for years, to find out if there’s something really there.” Those set of stories are there precisely to real-make the thing so it can be protected and registered with priors as meaningful. The danger here occurs when the protective process seals the cluster off to outside feedback due to sealing itself off.

Crucially, the main things driving the truth-making orientation on a global scale, meaning an orientation set outside the current frame and operating at a higher level of abstraction, are higher order targets for accuracy and correspondence with reality (targets that stay stabilized long-term), alongside a willingness to expose local frames to constraints they cannot control. It means having the ability to ensure field configurations reach a stable attractor state in which targets aiming for truth exist and matter to the system. Ironically, we must “real-make” goals of truth-seeking and error-correction so that correspondence with reality becomes an active priority within the system. This is long since why I have believed that learning and “reach towards” truth is more of a spiritual endeavor than an intellectual pursuit as it requires sources from within that must summon the courage to surrender control and look outside established priors that exist only to protect the current model. This is why a certain amount of slack, like felt safety, is needed for quality truth-making: without it, the system will simply prioritize protecting deep priors which would be expensive to update. Externalizing a local frame to expose it to constraints it cannot control or plan for costs slack, since it effects the system's ability to commit or not commit to certain configurations. So orientation towards truth is partly a matter of affordability, not just values or domain. A system that cannot afford exposure to external feedback due to low slack will produce real-making that feels like truth-making, like when someone doubles down on a belief when stressed.
