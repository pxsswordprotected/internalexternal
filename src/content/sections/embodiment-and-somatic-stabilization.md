I want to expand on the importance of the body in this framework and explain in more detail how it connects to the load-bearing tree, the Energetic Triad, and the three layers by showing how the field is physically held and enacted in the body.

First, a definition: a haptic contraction is a felt tightening or clenching in bodily sensation, whether muscular, visceral, or vascular. It is the somatic form that a defended or violated prediction arising from prior structures takes in direct experience. Its counterpart, expansion, is simply the release of this kind of contraction. In this sense, contraction refers more specifically to tension that participates in maintaining or responding to an active field configuration. It is unclear if ALL bodily tension can be mapped to some type of priors-relationship.

(One possible mechanistic account is Michael Edward Johnson’s “vasocomputation” hypothesis, which proposes that active-inference predictions may be physically instantiated through vascular tension. In this account, a contraction first collapses an ambiguous neural pattern into a more definite one, holds it as a form of medium-term memory, and, if sustained, latches into a durable prior that resists updating until the prediction resolves or the latch is released. Through this frame, taṇhā, or craving, is this compression-clench, which is why craving and the somatic contraction that holds it can be understood as the same process expressed through different channels.)

These haptic contractions may match the depth of the priors being held, meaning that deeper priors produce stronger, more persistent, and “physically deeper” contractions. For example, a deep, load-bearing prior held as tension is trunk-level, which is one reason chronic contractions can be so hard to release: releasing them means changing a foundational structure that experience relies on. Tacit knowledge becomes an obvious example here. Deeply conditioned priors can be expressed and stabilized through recurring haptic contraction patterns so they can be enacted. These patterns do not instantiate only single priors, but instead help instantiate whole field configurations, each of which may come with a characteristic pattern of haptic contraction and release that supports its properties like targets and constraints. These patterns may also keep the targets associated with those priors active after the original situation has passed so they can be resolved. By holding unresolved prediction or target error in the embodied layer, they keep attention and action weighted toward signals that might reduce the discrepancy or satisfy the target. In this sense, haptic contraction can function like an embodied bookmark, keeping later perception and action organized around an unfinished target so the error can be reduced. Importantly, a bidirectional feedback loop is running here. Active priors can generate contraction, while the resulting interoceptive signals provide new evidence that the predicted state is real and meaningful. This develops a feedback loop in which the body helps maintain the field that originally organized the body. Episodic recall here is doing something similar in which reinstating the configuration dealing with the memory, the internal representations, reinstates its somatic pattern, since this is how the configuration is enacted.

If we take a look at a mathematician in the middle of solving a problem, their tacit knowledge, embodied through conditioned patterns of haptic contraction, is activated by the current problem and target to form a specific field configuration that comes with its own unique properties. Each new representation, such as a mental image, is then tested against the embodied felt sense of that field, with a negative difference in felt tone signaling that it does not fit and a positive difference signaling fit.

(Again I turn to the vasocomputation hypothesis as well as this tweet by Michael Johnson himself that proposes something very similar, which I did not see until after I put together the above paragraph. He describes these recurring patterns of muscular and vascular tension as “stances,” which closely parallels the claim here that field configurations have characteristic somatic patterns that stabilize predictions and make certain actions available.)

Some tension can be released within seconds because it is associated with priors on higher, less load-bearing branches of the tree, while other tension remains stable across many situations because it is associated with deeper, more foundational priors. Noticeable bodily contraction is the somatic expression of a negative difference in felt tone because it arises when the system registers input that conflicts with a held prior or attempts to prevent that prior from being updated. The magnitude and persistence of the contraction may scale with how deep and load-bearing the prior is. When input conflicts with a deep prior, the resulting negative difference in felt tone can be especially strong because updating that prior would require revising many of the structures built on top of it.

Taken together, these three accounts describe the same process from different sides: the load-bearing tree gives the structure its depth, the Energetic Triad explains the magnitude of the resulting spikes, and haptic contraction provides a physical substrate through which the structure is stabilized and enacted. Depth in the tree predicts how costly a prior is to update and how firmly its associated tension is latched. This means the meditation/contemplative work of releasing contraction and the epistemic work of updating knowledge may be closely related mechanistically, since both loosen the precision and embodied reinforcement that keep a load-bearing prior stable, making the underlying model more available for updating.

As stated earlier, single releases of contraction usually affect higher branches of the load-bearing tree, but repeated releases can gradually propagate downward as the field learns that the contraction is no longer necessary. With greater sensory clarity and regulatory slack, meditation allows the system to remain with prediction error without immediately restoring the old contraction through active inference. Over time, this can reduce the precision and embodied reinforcement of deeper priors, making increasingly load-bearing patterns available for updating. This is the same general process through which tacit knowledge develops, but applied in the opposite direction: repeated experience can both embody useful structures and loosen embodied structures that no longer serve their original function. Indeed, this mechanism helps explain why meditation can occasionally produce psychedelic-like moments through the release of deep tension, as psychedelics similarly loosen these rigid, load-bearing priors.

Connecting this to the representational layer, all representations bring their own properties into our field and reweight active priors. A representation imports properties into the field, and the body enacts them via active inference, so the somatic change is downstream of the field re-tuning, not the representation mysteriously touching muscle. It’s important to remember that these changes that the representation brings to the field are typically more surface level, affecting prediction branches that sit higher up, so the somatic changes it produces are usually the fast, unlatched kind (you tense or relax in the moment, and it releases when the representation drops), whereas the deep latched contractions that come from deep priors don’t change just because you adopted a new representation. For example, a representation of safety won’t do much if the body remains braced since interoceptive evidence still signals danger. Reframing can shift your state of being momentarily but doesn’t dissolve deep somatic holding unless that framing is held until it can sink deeper into the load-bearing tree. Now, nuance here is needed since there are instances in which representations, over time, can indeed affect the system deeply, like the practice of ideal parent figure protocol developed by Dr. Daniel P. Brown and David Elliott. In this instance, repeated imagery of attuned caregivers gives the field a stable representation of secure attachment that can be reinstantiated across sessions. In the terms of this framework, repeatedly using that representation while it produces an embodied sense of safety may gradually reduce the precision of older attachment predictions and train the new pattern(s) into the embodied layer, allowing an initially temporary representation to influence deeper priors and their coupled contractions. This results in a new field configuration and destabilizes the old attractor state and its basin, allowing for more flexibility. Importantly, time and repetition alone are not sufficient for a representation to reach deeper priors. The representation must generate trusted evidence and support actions that repeatedly produce outcomes the system can use to override its older predictions. So, representations re-tune the embodied layer when they are used, the body enacts the imported field via active inference, and this typically operates at the higher levels of branches in the load-bearing tree unless done so at a large enough time scale.

Then moving to the notational layer, we must first remember anything in this layer requires compression of at least the embodied layer. Insight originates as an embodied felt sense and gets compressed into representation or notation, such as Einstein’s “muscular type” thoughts: “the above-mentioned elements are, in my case, of visual and some of muscular type.” Now, since the embodied layer is so involved with this process of bringing forth notation, and we have established that priors are physically held as tension/contraction, then the physical intuition behind these insights is the body’s stored prior structure coming alive as a felt sense. So embodiment-in-the-layer-sense and embodiment-in-the-somatic-sense are the same substrate doing the same thing. We can come to an implied conclusion that pure symbol interaction with no grounded intuition produces no genuine insight. Because notation is always a lossy translation of the embodied, with no felt sense driving the interaction, there’s nothing being compressed and no positive difference in felt tone guiding which interactions are meaningful. We’ve all felt this before: we are reading something, and five minutes later we realize we have not retained almost a single thing because no loop is running to get feedback from our embodied layer; it’s just pure symbol input. This does not mean notional manipulation is useless without the embodied layer, since notation can function as external scaffolding and generate structures that can become embodied later. It’s that notation become meaningful to the system through feedback with the representational and embodied layers rather than through notation alone.

Just as an example, we can take the names OpenAI vs ClosedAI. Both these words import different properties into the field, like open being expansive and safe and closed being contracted and guarded, along with all the implications associated with each that propagate through the system. The priors associated with “open” and “closed” run deep and are activated “automatically” here, since the notation is so common that its implications have been trained into the field over lots of repetition. These properties re-tune the field, which is felt somatically by using different patterns of tension to lean closer to open or closed. The field is embodied, so a representation that imports branches associated with “closed” produces actual haptic contraction, and one that imports branches associated with “open” produces a sense of release, even if very subtly. That said, the notation of “open” or “closed” can bring about representations that affect the field, but not always, since the priors associated with open and closed are deep enough to instantiate on their own without a deliberate representation being placed, which is why the embodied orientation can happen before you have consciously pictured anything. Keep in mind all of this is related to the individual and dependent on context and conditioning. “Open” can imply freedom in one field and vulnerability in another, while “closed” can imply restriction or protection depending on the active target.
