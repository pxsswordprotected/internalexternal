> "We feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble."
> 
> – William James, [*What Is an Emotion?*](https://emotion.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1353/2020/11/James_1884_What_is_an_Emotion.pdf)

What is an emotion? This question has been attempted to be answered [*a lot*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotion/)*,* along with their load-bearing downstream effects on our mind-body system. I'd like to offer an account of emotions that works within this framework that may point to a unique direction. Truthfully, any attempt to name an emotion or define them is a failure due to moving up the compression gradient which leaves characteristics left out, but nonetheless attempts are still useful in their own right.

An emotion is a context-sensitive field configuration that structures signals from the body and environment, felt tone, conditioned concepts, targets, and actions into a unifying gestalt like fear or anger. What is felt in experience as an emotion is not a raw signal but the organization the field produced. Emotions are not just properties of the field because their construction is downstream of many properties coming together and forming a coherent generative structure. The purpose of emotions naturally arises out of its definition and placement in this framework. [It seems as though](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390700/) emotion concepts are resuable chunks that organize bodily and situational context which would normally be ambiguous. For example, fear is not just a stored object that gets released or expressed, it is more like a context-sensitive organizational tool reconstructed from prior structure. Researchers like to define emotion episodes which are more specifically temporally unfolding configurations that “[comprise different components such as appraisal of the situation, action preparation, physiological responses, expressive behavior, and subjective feelings](https://ppw.kuleuven.be/okp/_pdf/Scherer2019TEPEA.pdf)”, but this framework will just use the term emotion across the board. Crucially, emotions are not feelings. An emotion is a whole field configuration, while a feeling is how that configuration becomes known from the inside. Felt tone also is not a feeling because it is narrower, referring only to the configuration’s valence. For example, *warmth* is a feeling, but we would not consider this an emotion because it is not necessarily a structural category. To clarify terms further, mood must be distinguished as well. [Mood might be considered](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7149) as a slower background configuration that structures and biases how ongoing input gets interpreted. It does not require one absolute clear gestalt, and it can create feedback loops in which current felt tone influences perceived outcomes and how they feed back into mood.

Taken as the field contains many properties and mechanisms at play, such as sensory evidence, attention, context, and learned concepts from others, it is clearly bodily signals are not simply read and then labeled as a certain emotion. As the system attempts to infer what is going on with its current state and what to do next, all of these different things brew together which pulls the system in many different directions. [Ultimately, precision weighting helps determine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5062100/) which things, like sensory signals, exert the most influence on the field’s current interpretation and action.

Emotions are indeed gestalts. They compress related configurations rather than exact, formulaic structures. For example, moments of fear share enough properties across time and with enough repetition that fear becomes a deep structure built into the dependency tree, made up of priors leaning on the body and context. The instances of fear are chunked together as the system attempts to save capacity so it does not have to process each component of fear everytime they show up together. Crucially, instances of fear can slightly differ in contraction patterns, felt tone, attention, and action, but they are still chunked together as one gestalt, which can result in the "misclassification" of future instances due to wide pruning. And crucially, just as the compression gradient is a continuous unfolding of compression rather than being rigid, boundaries between [emotions are not discrete, they are fuzzy](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1702247114). This means that categorical emotions lie within a continuous space, connected by intermediate states rather than separated by hard boundaries. This suggests something like a structural-threshold modeling in the sense that the field gradually climbs through ambiguous configurations until one learned one organizes enough to become a clear structuring gestalt. This means "failed" emotional gestalts can exist, or ambiguous components that don't cohere into a whole. This can be seen at the beginnings of a felt sense about an experience: the feeling is there but it has yet to cohere into a nameable thing.

When it comes to targets, emotions complicate things. Not every emotion has just one target built in to its known configuration, though often times a singular target is what is perceived, like a need for safety known through its action policy such as "I need to get away from this situation" in episodes of fear. Emotions can be seen as a sort of modulator that relate and connect differing targets when it comes to current or predicted future input. They they organize the field in relation to these targets in such a way [that relate to preferred states, needs, and capacity](https://ppw.kuleuven.be/okp/_pdf/Scherer2019TEPEA.pdf), like how targets of anxiety might get someone to move to state of avoidance to ensure the system is not overwhelmed by bodily sensations. The most relevant target then in a given moment caused by a clear emotion has multiple ways of being known, such as being implicit, distributed, uncertain, or violated. Because of the overlap and nuance of targets when dealing with emotions, action policies are re-weighted without determining one fixed action across the board. This means an emotion may make a number of actions, like escaping or freezing, more reachable depending on the field, without any singular policy encapsulating the emotion itself. [A large contributor to this is context](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103113001728), which is why emotions can be so hard to analyze or know since context is always interpreted and organized in such a way by the field. When it comes to targets and their action policies, precision matters since it can be distributed across a wide net of things across the field like sensory evidence, emotions, threats, and available actions. Because of this, high precision on action policies of an emotion can coexist with the belief that the action will have any meaningful outcome at all. My system can place high precision on the action of running from a situation to avoid the sensations, but in the background the belief that this is not a long-term solution can still exist.

When it comes to emotions, the embodied layer is crucial. Emotions are embodied, meaning bodily changes are not just a result of a thing that was created entirely in the brain first. Sensations like tension and heart rate have the ability to influence the emotional experience and provide interoceptive evidence the system uses to predict and regulate its state, like through homeostatic priors. The body is essentially a part of the field configuration that is producing the emotion itself; they cannot be decoupled. As stated in the field(?) section, the dependency tree says most deep priors are universally shared because they are innate or heavily conditioned across the human species, while higher level ones differ due to experience and training data. Accordingly, it can be said that the human body itself is one of the most universally shared structures, so because of this, processes like interoception and action preparation are constrained in very similar ways before an emotion gestalt can organize them through the field. The body provides universal active constraints that limit our processes that determine perception and action. When it comes to an emotional category itself, these are things that sit higher in the dependency tree than these deeper priors and processes, which means they are more malleable to the system. The gestalt built on top of the lower levels can vary much more, which [maps cross-culturally](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw8160). Culture can shape which chunks are available within the emotional configuration, like what actions are socially permitted and who is the type of person to commit the action itself. Taking anger as an example, it very clearly stays at the top of our body as to activate readiness and the needed actions associated with anger, such as confrontation, which is determined by what we can do with our bodies. This is where conditioning ends up mattering like through culture and environment, since repetition and training data will influence what action and high-level organizations are available when the emotion structures experience. And as it turns out, [conditioning is very important](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876074/) in determining what exactly we do with these high level, malleable organizations. It is also important to point out that [increased knowledge through language and concepts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29399639/) can allow the field to more readily distinguish between states rather than compressing nuanced ones into. It turns out that increasing knowledge can reduce unhappiness through removing barriers between attractor states, allowing us to move more freely among them.

This makes the somatic connection quite clear: a field configuration that is heavily organized around an emotion is held and enacted in the body like any other, such as tacit knowledge. Sensory evidence that is a result of the high level organization, such as posture and interoceptive signals, creating a feedback loop in which the system confirms the existing organization to be "correct" in its way of describing experience. This is why habitual emotional responses become available so quickly and feel like how things "just are" rather than being actively constructed. These are conditioned patterns held in the body, the same substrate carrying other knowledge, and these patterns can differ in whether it is held with rigidity or making a wide range of policies available. It's important to mention contraction here, in the phenomenological sense of "closing down", can play many roles such as closing off available actions or maintenance of emotional attractors. For instance, it might be tempting to label grief as purely contractive sense it is a thing held in the body, but there can exist expansive grief in which it is not held with rigidity and the emotion moves through the system without blockage.

What's going on with mixed emotions? It seems as though mixed emotions are those that the high-level structure produces many mixed chunks, like grouping related sadness and happiness properties together. Multiple target relations can remain active at the same time, causing the system to interpret these as singular things being mixed together rather than a whole new structuring gestalt. Just to point back to section 4.1, this exact thing happens when a math problem contains individually understood pieces with no single organizing structure underlying them, which results in several partial readings of the problem instead of a coherent whole (relationships and all), which causes confusion. Mixed emotions of course do not require two fields, since the one field can contain targets that are being satisfied, while some are being violated. As an example, if you are graduating college, there might be satisfaction of targets pertaining to life achievements but violation of targets in relation to identity and friendship, both are valid and result in seemingly contradicting interpretations.

Connecting this to expression and the notational layer of the compression gradient, expression itself can be an "inaccurate" (from the outside) indicator as to what the field is holding. Notation is extremely compressed, so one instance cannot fully capture the vast landscape powering the mind-body system. This becomes especially complicated when it comes to mixed emotions since one behavior can be compatible with many contradicting internal chunks, which we then typically interpret as the behavior being only about one unified thing, when indeed this is usually not the case. There may be "leakage" in the system in which suppressed or mixed states may affect subtle signals like posture or voice, but they are not guaranteedß to be known through one external signal.

Regulation of emotions in this framework can be seen as being a part of the field configuration rather than a separate system controlling an emotion object. It can be a tool used to do many different things like changing precision about an interpretation or action, or take different bodily action that results in some type of attenuation of the emotional state, such as deep breathing when anxious. Reappraisal (changing how you interpret a situation to change how it feels) of a situation that is "causing" an emotion can also be helpful in regulation since it changes how the organization is known and done. This differs from suppression since suppression leaves the existing organization from an emotion alive and only blocks what the expression looks like. Hence why reappraisal and suppression have much different downstream effects; the ladder is much more harmful in the long run. Suppression is also a somatic act, such as tensing muscles, which eventually forms deep latched contraction which ends up producing suffering as seen later in section 5.3.
