> "We feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble."
> 
> – William James, [*What Is an Emotion?*](https://emotion.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1353/2020/11/James_1884_What_is_an_Emotion.pdf)

What is an emotion? This question has been attempted to be answered [*a lot*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotion/)*,* along with their load-bearing downstream effects on our mind-body system. I'd like to offer an account of emotions that works within this framework that may point to a unique direction. Truthfully, any attempt to name an emotion or define them is a failure due to moving up the compression gradient which leaves characteristics left out, but nonetheless attempts are still useful in their own right.

An emotion is a context-sensitive field configuration that structures signals from the body and environment, felt tone, conditioned concepts, targets, and actions into a unifying gestalt like fear or anger. What is felt in experience as an emotion is not a raw signal but the organization the field produced. Emotions are not just properties of the field because their construction is downstream of many properties coming together and forming a coherent generative structure. Researchers like to define emotion episodes which are more specifically temporally unfolding configurations that “[comprise different components such as appraisal of the situation, action preparation, physiological responses, expressive behavior, and subjective feelings](https://ppw.kuleuven.be/okp/_pdf/Scherer2019TEPEA.pdf)”, but this framework will just use the term emotion across the board. Crucially, emotions are not feelings. An emotion is a whole field configuration, while a feeling is how that configuration becomes known from the inside. Felt tone also is not a feeling because it is narrower, referring only to the configuration’s valence. For example, *warmth* is a feeling, but we would not consider this an emotion because it is not necessarily a structural category. To clarify terms further, mood must be distinguished as well. [Mood might be considered](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms7149) as a slower background configuration that structures and biases how ongoing input gets interpreted. It does not require one absolute clear gestalt, and it can create feedback loops in which current felt tone influences perceived outcomes and how they feed back into mood.

Taken as the field contains many properties and mechanisms at play, such as sensory evidence, attention, context, and learned concepts from others, it is clearly bodily signals are not simply read and then labeled as a certain emotion. As the system attempts to infer what is going on with its current state and what to do next, all of these different things brew together which pulls the system in many different directions.
