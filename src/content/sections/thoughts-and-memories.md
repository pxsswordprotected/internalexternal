Folk categories, taxonomies of things named by their ordinary usage rather than their theoretical shaping, in this framework refer to three different things in general that share the same underlying shape: thoughts, memories, and emotions. These categories, again named by mass culture, bringing the notation layer into focus, name property clusters of the same thing rather than entirely different things with no relations. What they name is that of gestalts, of the pin-point construction of things that have differing properties. It seems as though ["thought" and "memory"](https://philarchive.org/rec/ANDADF-3?utm_source=chatgpt.com) are two interpretations of [similar constructions, both sharing mechanics](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioral-and-brain-sciences/article/abs/on-the-constructive-episodic-simulation-of-past-and-future-events/4857CCE46631D0DBC017B7F45AB4028A), but showing up differently in their final form and characteristics. As mentioned, emotions is similar in this aspect, but this will be touched upon later.

First we must answer, what is thought? This being a matter of definitional curiosity rather than metaphysical, this framework takes a specific stance. A thought is a gestalt that can be attended to as a discrete object, that is decoupled from present input, and carrying the semantics of what is compressed. Taking the example of a thought about a blue dog, this thought is a discrete object (a thing), decoupled from what is going on, that is, physical reality does not represent this thought, and it carries the semantics of "blue" and "dog" through language and associative images. When "decoupled from present input" is spoken of, it is not meant to negate the fact that thoughts have their own properties and influence over the field. Thoughts are still an organizing gestalts that can take hold of the field which then has effects on the mind and body and the three layers of the compression gradient. A mental image is a thought that holds a medium that lies in the representational layer, for instance. However, the system itself does not interpret this "decoupling" as a hard boundary, as a binary. This is why a thought of something anxiety-inducing can raise your heart rate: active inference treats this gestalt as it would input from the "physical world."

It is important to mention the boundaries of this decoupling from physical reality can become murky. For instance, a gut feeling about someone or a situation in front of you, which is a part of the embodied layer, is input driven, but thoughts produced out of this are still decoupled in the sense that they are not attached or tagged onto the physical layer.

When speaking of thoughts carrying semantics about what is being compressed, the sense of "aboutness" of a thing comes from compression itself, since a gestalt points to what is being compressed through organization. "Water bottle" is about bottles because it organizes bottle relations and associative properties. As mentioned in the field section, the system contains numerous scales of attention and awareness, like amodal perception being extremely implicit and baked into our system in which it is extremely difficult to notice. Due to this, most "products" of cognitive processes do not become thoughts because they are not discrete objects. You never have a thought about the back of your hand being there because this process is so baked in and deep. It is also extremely hard to catch this cognitive mechanism taking place and turning it into a discrete thought.

Getting more abstract, having a thought and noticing a thought, self-awareness of thoughts, is not coming from an outside privileged point of view. It's just that when we think we have noticed a thought, our model of perception of thoughts becomes as though there is something separate having an outside perspective of the thing rather than being a part of the thing itself. Self-awareness about thoughts exist precisely because the inference move activates priors dealing with selfhood, like being a separate thing outside of object-like thoughts. Thoughts themselves and a thinker differ in persistence and depth, not in medium. The observer, the thinker, the doer, is another gestalt, the same kind of the thoughts themselves. How could it be anything else? Because of this, thoughts seem to multiply the moment you actively start participating in them. Hence why rumination and overthinking are thought-loops reinstating clusters of field-configurations, like when going over a conversation in your head late at night which brings online the associated state.

As Michael Levin puts it, "I think we are massive, self-persistent thought patterns (in metabolic, anatomial, behavioral, and other media). We spawn off smaller ones, and host others (some which have agendas and different degrees of competency). It's thoughts all the way down (and up). The distinction between patterns in excitable media and "real things", between data and machine, between thought and thinker, is a false dichotomy - just names for parts of a spectrum." In this way, Levin positions thoughts as a sort of nested hierarchical connected network, with some thoughts having seemingly more agency (like having more degrees of competency). What this means is that some thoughts might be more animated than others, in the sense that it draws more responses from all three layers of the compression gradient, which then has downstream effects like the field interpreting an embodied response of thoughts as a marker that the thought is more "real" than if it did not have an embodied response, or thoughts seemingly spawning differing levels of complexity of internal representations, like simulations.

Speaking to the hierarchical model and depth of thoughts, one aspect of this is that they can sit at every level of the dependency tree. For instance, a passing image, which sits shallow and far out in the tree's branches, vs. the background ongoing sense of being a person, a structure that underpins most thoughts, which sits very deep near the trunk. This also speaks to the differing levels of attention and awareness mentioned in the field section. Moment-to-moment attention influences thoughts through small temporal variations of thoughts about properties of things. This room is shaped this way, this sound is like this, this sensation in my foot feels like this. Reweighting these "fast" properties in every moment changes our thoughts by effecting the field, not thought content directly. Then you have dwelling attention– resting attention on a thought has the ability to retune the field, creating a feedback loop between thoughts and perception. Lastly there is sculpting through attention, which here is the act of sinking thoughts and their associative field configurations deep into the embodied layer, like repeatdly thinking the same things which makes those thoughts more available and the associative characteristics of those thoughts more engrained.

All of this is possible because thoughts and mind-objects are generated, not only fetched. If they were just fetched from past experiences, it would be impossible to have thoughts of things never occurring, like fantasy or thoughts of the future. You've never seen a blue dog in real life, but you can easily generate a mind-object of a blue dog, which can be attended to and is decoupled from current input outside of the thought. This has important implications, like the field being a core contributor to what thoughts can be generated in the first place, since they are constrained by factors like priors and context, not infinitely random. Equally as important is the implications this has about memory.

Getting into memory, its process can be defined as learning persistent learned dispositions that change what the system can reproduce. See how thoughts and memories seem to be folk categories of similar mechanisms? Recalling what you had for breakfast, for instance, carries compressed images and language that is associated with a temporal marker of "this morning." It can be said then that the essence of [a thing being a memory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5404722/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) is itself a [cluster of properties](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5312673/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), recalled gestalts dealing with temporality and familiarity. [Déjà vu](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-8721.2008.00605.x), an interesting case here, carries the ["familiar" bodily markers](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22322010/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), that of embodied signals, but it does not match the temporal content of what a memory should be as the input lies in the present moment which differs from the cluster of things we would normally call the past and a memory. An objection that might come up is "something is clearly persistent though, what is it?" What persists in what we call memory is a disposition the field holds towards the regeneration of the configuration dealing with the memory, not content itself. Memories hold structural changes that are about conditions of reproduction. As an example, learning to ride a bike changes what your body can reproduce, dealing with the embodied layer; it is not a stored film sitting in a container waiting to be pulled out.

[To speak of memories is to recognize that things, dispositions, have to be accessed](https://linkinghub.elsevier.com/retrieve/pii/S0022537166800488). This means something must persist, but what? Here, this persistence *is* memory, meaning, the medium by which things travel is landscapes, not specific states. Basins, field configurations, are a consequence of parameters being tuned, not a thing ready to be accessed independently of context. Take a model of a spider tower, but instead of rubber bands, it uses reverberating strings that responds to weight. This tower has levels which represents the hierarchical model of priors that structure experience, in which priors that sit in the trunk of the dependency tree sit at the top of the tower (imagine input as a ball being dropped into the tower, first being processed through deep priors like "thing-ness"). Now, anytime a string is pulled back and let go, whether through input or external cues, it reliably produces a ripple effect throughout the system, which produces certain outputs. These outputs are not stored anywhere "inside" of the strings themselves, rather, the outputs are a result of  the properties inside the layers of strings like their tension and length. The output is what the properties produce when an event occurs, when we pull some strings back and let them interact. If we adjust a string or a layer, such as its length, we get a different output. What occurred was not the direct editing of an output object, which would be a statically stored memory, but the editing of the structure that generated the object, the memory, such as bodily context. In this model, if we take a ball being dropped inside as input, it is crucial to recognize that the ball's traversal through the system has the ability to change the structure as well. The tower retunes itself as it is used, it not only is changed through external factors. One string hitting another, due to input making it move, which over time alters the impacted string, is the tower changing itself for instance.

To make clear what the role exactly is of storage in terms of memory and dispositions, it should be viewed through the lens of cascading and nested connective schemas. When the spider tower model is spoken of, something still must be stored in order for it to be used. What is being stored then is better thought of as "data structures" and "algorithms" that can record durable changes. Synapses, connectivity, excitability, and associative engram relations are different ways these data structures and algorithms take hold of our system, which is something like changing the tension or length of a string in the spider tower. Memory then can be thought of here as a compressed set of dispositions, while conscious memory, like active recall, is construction from these sets. Construction here might be better thought of as RE-construction, as we are decompressing content from "past" structure which is compressed itself from the field, to result in information seemingly coming from the past. As laid out in the compression gradient section, compression naturally leaves out details, so when out system compresses information that is encoded "inside memory," it naturally biases towards the present field configuration. In this way, [we access traces left by past experience, not the past event itself](https://youtu.be/c8iFtaltX-s?si=EkdkRFPqEAO1iB3u). What would it mean if memory was the exact event? How would we practically survive if not for this compression?

If we were to view memory through a "pulling from isolated storage" lens (a common, incorrect model of memory), then the storage size would need to directly scale with memory itself as it grows, like more "storage slots" increasing. The view would also predict that new memories "crowd out" old ones, making them proportionally less in impact than what they were. However, the view of memory in this framework predicts that storage does not have to scale linearly with the number of things memorized, and new learning actually distorts old recall through cascading effects rather than simply making old recall disappear due to crowding out. This is precisely why learning something new can interact with old memories and change their foundation, because the structure itself was never isolated. Changing the strings, the structure, of production fundamentally alters its output, instead of old memories simply being untouched through new learning. This is precisely why a thought can be had without the object of the thought being stored in memory like a blue dog: a stored lookup table cannot produce that, rules and structure can, constrained by the field.

Because recall is reconstruction, which is also recall, our current context and situation has the ability to bias the memory in a certain direction. Memories then have the ability to change over-time due to re-interpretation and the underlying structure changing from which it is generated, like built priors. The current body, environment, targets, and model therefore have an active role in influencing memories. Memories become constrained by what we are already predisposed to, which makes our current action and perception all the more important. If we so choose to indulge in behavior that biases towards interpretations of hatred or threat, this has the ability to affect our memories in such a way that might produce more suffering than needed. Recall is an active process, something to be protected in the sense that it is important to understand how our current field is exposing us to certain interpretations and behaviors which can decay what is important to us. Ultimelty, storage here explains continuity, the sense that something is persistant across time, while recall and active memory explain why the memory and structure can change. This is what Michael Levin means when he says "you don't have access to the past. What you have access to are the memory traces, the engrams, that have been left by your interaction with the past in your mind, your brain and body… and now it's up to you to reinterpret it. And you might reinterpret it in exactly the same way, but you might not. And you don't have to — you don't have any allegiance to that." You might reinterpret the traces differently due to the structure being different in the present moment compared to when the input of the memory was experienced.

The perception of recall, like the evaluation if a memory has been accessed successfully, is dependent on the target you're using to evaluate whether recall has been successful. As mentioned in the field section, these targets are often arbitrary, learned and generated through specific conditioning. For instance, when judging our ability to memorize a speech, it is dependent on how much of the speech we should have memorized. If my target is to fully memorize all of the words in the correct order, anything else is considered a failure, not correctly memorized. Failing to memorize the entire speech would be considered target error. This is intuitive but has interesting implications. For instance, standard testing is taking a frame, a target of memorizing things which is dependent on the instructor's targets themselves. If you were to get a multiple choice question wrong, you would get a binary wrong or right, even if the choice you chose was close in relation to the correct answer. Ability to memorize things is not always a binary yes or no, it is dependent on the framing effects you place upon the output. Targets also matter in categorizing the identity of memories, which will be discussed at the end here.

In this sense, our view of memory cannot be separated from culture and enviornment. This has the ability to change the structure of memory itself, but changing what we attend to and how we organize information. This is made clear when you realize repeated use of certain practices, like writing, has an ability to influence specific characteristics of how we attend to and recall memories. The practice of writing about memory leaves out details for instance, as laid out in the compression gradient, while a practice like photography, a practice that can be seen as "freezing" a memory in time through image, influence how we envision things. These different practices highlight certain aspects of memory that otherwise would be collapsed in the dependency tree. So, culture does not create the basic mechanisms of memory, but it has the ability to reshape organization and intepretation of what is remembered, which then feeds back into memory itself.

None of this negates retrieval itself. When we encounter the problem of multiplying 3x6, we do not necessarily need to actively recover the mechanism and structure of multiplication. The answer of 18 is intuitive, quick, and feels instantly produced. However, the argument is that the answer of 18 is retrieval-*like* not *statically stored.* Meaning, 3x6=18 is a chunk that is deep and easy to reach, given the input and cue of the notation "3x6." It reinstates a configuration, the structure we use to navigate the dependency tree, so reliably and so fast due to conditioning with no competing branches in the dependency tree showing up. This is fluency, the easy and reliable movement between these configurations and dispositions; the conditions for the reproduction of the state can be met easily. If storage was static and entered into a lookup tables as records, reproducing "3x6" would be just as easy as "7x8," [which is not the case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9054599/). This also predicts errors themselves are structured, meaning errors are dependent on the structure, the strings of the spider tower, which means clusters of chunks often become tangled. If one were to get 3x6 wrong, [it would be an answer like 24, rather than say, 156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17176659/). The same thing shows up when we attempt to recall memories: we do not mistake the memory for something fantastical that clearly do not represent real-life constraints; the mistakes we make in recall cluster around the same structures. Mistakes in recall are missed in related regions of strings in the spider tower, branches of the dependency tree, not a mistake in trying to access static, isolated storage. Releatdely, Memories can bend in a certain direction, meaning they can shift and be biased towards [your current mood](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10076454/) or felt identity. This then changes the structure underneath it, affecting future recall. This is the original bias persistening even after the mood has passed.Just as "any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic," any sufficiently advanced generative process of gestalts is indistinguishable from retrieval from storage.

Memories also have a reachability property, how easy it is to reach and enact right now. Interestingly, how strongly a memory can be retained can sometimes persist despite low reachability. Meaning, something can be well-learned and held in the system, while still being difficult to reach. When you "forget" a memory, or a cluster of inter-connected memories like a language, you are not forgetting its specific qualities, those are still "in you" inside generative structure. What is lost is your ability to go back and reach that structure; you cannot get to "there" from where you currently are. This is preceisly why spaced repeition is effective: reaching something when it is hard changes the structure that generated the memory. Reaching the memory when it is easy doesn't move the system due to low variability; the system need not learn anything new about its structure. Difficulty of recall can be due to low reachability when means retuning of the structure is occuring, which is not a bad thing. Hence why the act of cramming, attempting to memorize and learn new things within a very small amount of time feels good, and can work in the short-term, but is not very effective in deeply retuning the structure which lasts longer-term. To be clear, [memories can still be transformed or lost through active decay](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23369831/), meaning reachability alone does not explain why we forget certain things.

Another reason spaced repetition works is that context also matters for reachability, because the structure becomes sensitive to new factors that retune the structure itself like mood, environment, and social situations. If you review content through generation on different days, you get different contexts each time which means the structure strengthens across a wider field of variability; the memories become more reachable across the board from different starting points. This is basin widening, the same mechanism that occurs when trauma is learned and conditioned across more contexts if it is not kept in line. All of this works precicely because memory is not just opening a static file cabinet and pulling out needed info; the act of producing content is what strengthens memory, like writing things down rather than just re-reading notation.

The problem of isolated facts being memorized is an interesting problem in this framework due to the three layers of the compression gradient. What is meant by this is that, often times the way we evaluate memory is through a limited perspective of notation. For instance, flashcards typically deal with language, having a question and answer. What is perceived is that these are isolated nodes of notation, meaning memorizing one is added to our bank of memory. As laid out above this is not the case, and instead what is changed is generative structure. This is important for recognizing what it means to memorize something, because memorizing something means an affect across all three layers of the compression gradient. The memory of the fact that the USA is a country echoes throughout the layers. For instance in the embodied layer, patterns of vocal chord movements are now produced from the structure, an internal model is changed, and notation like writing is changed. This results in a system of change across varying levels of compression due to the field changing, which is what this framework already predicted. Crucially, a marker of change does not have to sit in the same layer as it comes from. "3x5" is perceived as notation, which then reverabtes across the layers and the field, but part of what "tells" you the answer is 15 comes from the embodied layer, like the feeling of counting itself or vocal chord movements. This is all a result of structure and its reverabting effects, not isolated nodes.

Remembering that structure can be broad, meaning "3x5" and "3x6" share fundamental structure but differ in their wide branches of used inference, certain structure can collapse options before effort is put in to remember something. For instance, "3x5" has multiplication structure that can be applied to a large field of cues, while someone's name does not neccrasily have the same structure. When trying to remember a person's name, there is little structure-sensitive rules that collapse options. So someone's face could be "Bob," "Kevin," or "John" all the same, while "3x5" immeditely collapases around hard-struck rules. "3x5" cannot be anything, which is why you don't get answers like "200," rather you might get an answer like "20." Nothing about a face or body constrains the product because there are no rules to be applied. There is no branch in the dependcy tree routing face features to name sounds, but there are branches routing numbers to how they interact with mathemtical rules. This is why arbitrary pairs with no underlying shared structure needs more repeition to be more asily accessed. And it is why mnemonics work: they provide shared branches that discriminate gestalts at the margins.

Seen through this lens, development of memory, crucial in expertise, can be seen as a parallel to the skill of sensory clarity, mentioned in the previous section. What this means is that memorizing "3x3" and "3x4" is the same move as de-chunking a sensation gestalt into working, smaller parts. Both of these are individuating structure at its fine points so the system can discern detailed differences. Both operations use and grow branches that come out of a shared structure.

Philosophically, it is interesting to consider the question as to what it means for a thing to be the same memory. If I hold a memory in one instance of time that produces certain embodied outputs, like a fast heart beat, then I recall the memory a year later and it produces different embodied outputs, like calmness, does this mean the memory is still the same memory? At what point does a memory fundamentally become a new thing? If I hold a memory one way in a given context, like when around certain people, but hold the same memory differently when I'm alone, is that the "same" memory? Most people would probably answer, yes that's the same memory, but why is this? What is being preserved? *Some* of the structure that produced the memory has to be changed sense the output is slightly different. Is it that the perceived *content* of the memory, like what is going on in the thought, is what matters? But if this were the case, we would have to exclude associative embodied feelings, decoupling them from the content, when in reality, these embodied feelings have a chance to change on their own, which then changes the content itself, like noticing new details we originally covered up. In a way, memories and thoughts can be seen as being "alive", in the sense that they respond to structure, which is a product of a multitude of interacting things like environment and bodily state, and error correct by responding to targets we hold. Because of this, we can never truly compare a memory to the original event, since the original event can never occur again with the same structure we were holding, nor can it occur in such a way to which we can "objectively" see it in its totality.

Because we can't compare a memory to its original causal event, and because memories error-correct through current targets, evaluating "sameness" of a memory across time is complicated. Let's say you form a memory at 18 years old, then a year later you recall it and it elicits a certain bodily response. Next, you recall the "same" memory at 20, and the bodily response is different. Now, checking if these are the same memories might depend on the targets you are using when recalling. If the target is something like "did this happen to me" then yes the memory would be the same. If the target is "does this memory produce suffering," then the answer would be no, this is not the same memory. So in a way, memories have their own way of reducing error prediction when trying to match the current field that is doing the recall. Targets are not the only thing at play though, since identity of memories also depends on relations, people, continuity, and location.  Because a memory is a disposition to regenerate a related field configuration, and that regeneration is dependent on current input, environment, mood, and other factors, the output is more like a context-sensitive process than a fixed object. The identity of such things in this framework can be seen as organizational, drawing different responses from the dependency tree, which then affects the field, which then loops back into the product.
