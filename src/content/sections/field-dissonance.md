There exists a mechanism that allows expression to differ from internal states. We can call this field dissonance, which occurs when conflicting properties or action policies remain active within the current field while the system must still settle on a workable organization or action. The field holds properties from multiple sources at once, like deep priors, the current bodily state, representations, and social constraints, and these can conflict with each other. Field dissonance is therefore broader than cognitive dissonance because the conflict does not need to occur between explicit beliefs. It can occur between bodily predictions, social constraints, representations, targets, and available actions.

For instance, a social constraint, which is downstream of deeper priors, could demand that you smile and say you are fine, while an embodied protective target makes withdrawal more salient. The field can hold both properties, but overt action compresses this complexity. A person may have several conflicting properties active while still having to enact one policy. When two policies require mutually exclusive actions, the system resolves the tension by acting on the more heavily weighted properties, leaving the violated ones unresolved.

The dominant policy may appear as cached such as the cliche statement of “doing good, how about you",” while the “losing” ones continue to influence expressions like posture and tone. Rather than simply disappearing, the violated property can “leak out” through automatic or involuntary behavior, making the dissonance externally observable. It can also remain active internally as somatic contraction and thoughts. This continued activity does not necessarily produce friction. Friction appears when the resulting state is more costly or difficult to integrate than predicted, while an unresolved target may otherwise persist as target error without producing a noticeable difference in felt tone. Though friction and negative felt tone is most common here. As another example of “leakage” we can take acts of lying to avoid conflict, where the story you tell stays surface-level while expressions like forced laughter and avoided eye contact expose the unintegrated target.

The weight of a property is a manifestation of precision weighting, which contributes to what gains control before an action occurs rather than just explaining the result after the fact. This is why dissonance phenomenologically feels like being right and wrong at the exact same time: one part of the field was satisfied while another part was violated through action. We can connect this to active inference and say that the field contains competing preferred states and policies, all attempting to coordinate the same limited channels of action. The conflict comes from multiple targets and demands remaining active within one changing field. 

However, resolution of conflicting properties is not limited to simply acting on the behavioral survivor. Because the system can reduce error either by changing the world through action or by updating its internal state, alternative resolution paths exist. Through weighting, one target can overpower the others. Through suppression, conflicting material can remain active while being kept away from control or awareness. The system can utilize reframing, like changing the narrative, to update the underlying model and reduce or remove the contradiction. It can resort to freezing, stopping action altogether when weights are stuck in uncertainty. Or it can try sequencing, which means separating the conflicting targets temporally, such as allowing ourselves to smile now to satisfy social priors and cry later to satisfy the embodied demand.

Another resolution method is that of integration through a structure preserving transformation. Instead of choosing to stay within a fantasy o safety for example, the system identifies the invariant properties inside the fantasy that are load-bearing and develops a policy that can be enacted in the real world that satisfies them alongside practical constraints. This reduces dissonance because conflicting target structures can partially resolve rather than compete. Integration is therefore another kind of structural threshold. Istead of selecting between existing policies to reduce conflict, the field produces a new gestalt of action that preserves important properties protected by the targets. For example, a math student struggling with an abstract proof stops choosing between rote memorization and blind guessing, instead constructing a visual geometric model that satisfies both the strict logical axioms and their need for spatial reasoning.

This can help explain why our words and behavior can diverge. It is a matter of the field reweighting its properties incredibly fast rather than two parallel fields running in the background. One major form of this is a conflict between a local target, like present comfort, and a global target, like maintaining long term health by going to the gym. A local target organizes action across a narrow horizon, while a global target coordinates outcomes across a wider span of time and context. The distinction is a gradient rather than a binary, ranging from targets that organize the next few seconds to those maintained across years.

A global target is not a property sitting in the field continuously. It is a label applied across time as the field repeatedly reinstantiates a target over a long time. In one moment you may be acting toward that goal because its relevant properties are weighted heavily, but a few moments later your actions can betray your words because the field has reconfigured toward immediate comfort. Failure to reach global targets is therefore not necessarily irrationality. It can be a local target temporarily gaining more motivational precision than the wider target.

Self control, then, is not simply overpowering parallel desires but keeping the global target alive and weighted in the field long enough to act on it. Discipline is repeatedly making the global target active and weighting it heavily enough to act on before the field defaults to a local target. For nuance, the global target should not always dominate. A long term target can be poorly calibrated, while a local target may contain information that should update it. Discipline is the ability to reliably reinstate a wider horizon target when short horizon competitors become salient, without becoming unable to revise targets when conditions change. A key goal here to cultivate discipline is to ensure the attractor states that are relevant for your goal are have high reachability. This means designing your environment and routines so that the path from your current state to the intended action requires minimal friction, while blocking off paths toward short-horizon defaults.

Attractors make conflicts asymmetrical. One target may be seriously weighted while the opposing field configuration has a wider or deeper basin. A person can consciously target calm while repeatedly returning to anxiety because the anxiety configuration is easier to reinstate and makes protective policies, like somatic contraction more reachable.

Field dissonance itself is not identical to suffering. It describes the broader situation in which active properties cannot all be satisfied together. The suffering loop begins when one of those demands becomes a highly weighted need that present experience must be different, while the preferred state remains difficult to reach.
