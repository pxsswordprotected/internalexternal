Now that we have established that any gestalt can in principle be held transparently, that they are made of causes and conditions no different from optical illusions, it is now a question of how to use this insight wisely. This of course is a highly debated topic already, for example what ethics argues about, but I want to give the answer that best fits this framework.

First we must differentiate between sanity and wisdom as they work together to provide maximal wellbeing and flourishing. Sanity is defined as holding gestalts with enough compression to take action and enough transparency to update, while having the ability to move between levels of abstraction when the situation calls for it. Wisdom is that same skill oriented towards targets that are worth weighting and whose subsequent actions matter. Sanity is a prerequisite for wisdom rather than an independent property, as you cannot select targets freely from inside a field you have no clear view of.

> “\[Consciousness of abstracting can be defined as] awareness that in our process of abstracting we have left out characteristics”
> — Alfred Korzybski

> Sanity \= Lucidly climbing up & down the branches of abstraction
>
> A big source of confusion I spot in people is when they get stuck attending to reality at stifling levels of abstraction; either by going too complex or too simple, and not being able to scale to a different level when that would get them out of a confuddlement.
>
> “Everything is Yin and Yang! No, it’s all One! No, it’s neither, it’s non-dual! But my five family members are coming over for dinner and I need to remember to buy 14 items at the supermarket, and calculate my taxes. And my brother is probably going to bring up politics which is so overwhelming complicated! But it’s all One...”
>
> So many minds get stuck trying to figure out the forest, when maybe what they really need to do is look at the individual trees. Or sometimes they need to get down to ground level and pick through the dirt, or sometimes take a bird’s eye view of the canopy, except they’re stuck or don’t realise everything else has moved on.
>
> The mind chunks and fuses reality all the time - you may have noticed. But how much have you really noticed, and how much flexible control do you have over this process? At least become lucid of it.
>
> I have found that by recognizing this feature of mind, and becoming better at navigating strata of abstraction, my sense of clarity and sanity has gone way up.
>
> Great spiritual development is to be able to nimbly both reach lower on the levels of abstractions - all the way to zero\* - AND be able to hold onto higher and higher levels of abstraction, extending our consciousness capacity, before we project to infinity (aka ‘more than I can make sense of’).
>
> — Roger Thisdell

The abstraction ladder operates as a continuous hierarchical spectrum of knowledge across experience, acting as an interface for different levels of the dependency tree. Moving up the ladder is an act of compression by dropping details into high-level patterns which collapses a dense gestalts. The inverse is decompression, moving down the ladder. This is decompressing a summary into its working parts and details. Neither end of the ladder is superior since they both come with trade-offs depending on the context and target at hand. Compression gives you strategic orientation, while decompression gives you specific available action. You can see this pattern in goals: the goal is the high-level global organization tool that orients our bow-tie(s), and the details of the goal are what set the actions to get there. When the mind-body system loses the fluid capacity to move between levels, confusion and suffering sets in and we try to navigate certain contexts with the wrong level of compression in mind, like getting buried in details when all we need is a high-level overview.

Speaking of goals, we can ground sanity in the bow-tie architecture. Each level of abstraction offers different affordances due to the way the dependency tree branches from and into gestalts. For instance, seeing a water bottle as just a water bottle offers us practical utility: we can pick it up, throw it away, or drink from it. Or, "placing" a frame of "physics" onto the bottle offers us insight into how its center of mass shifts as the water shifts, which predicts when it will tip over. Moving up or down this ladder of abstraction changes the resolution of the bottleneck in the bow tie serving the current target. If my target is "pick the bottle up," it's going to be best for me to look at that bottle as a practical tool because of those affordances the gestalt offers. These differing levels change which invariants are kept as stable and which details are dropped. As section 1.5 laid out, the target sets what the bottleneck needs to preserve, so the "right" level we work from is the one whose invariants fit the target. Confusion can be seen as a mismatch between the target and the level of abstraction you're working at: wanting to pick up a water bottle and looking at it through a purely physics lens is a mismatch. Knowledge domains like biology or physics are compressions other people put into external notation and we interpret through our own bow tie(s). Each domain hands us a different set of invariants we can work from, and so a different set of affordances for reaching a target.

However, flexibility between abstraction levels by itself does not guarantee sanity, since a mind-body system can be infinitely flexible within a closed loop of input. When a gestalt becomes large enough to structure every piece of input, it creates a rigid loop where every piece of counter-evidence is integrated as confirmation instead of error. [Fixation holds the gestalt as solid, and denial turns counter-evidence into confirmation or is explained away.](https://meaningness.com/fixation-and-denial)

Inside this closed loop, we receive lots of internal resonance because our interpretations feel right due to rigid structuring. However, resonance is a signal of unexpected lower integration cost; it says nothing of the system’s level of corresponding to reality. This is why sanity depends on open loops. Without constraints that the field could not have produced on its own, there is no way to tell a model that corresponds with reality from one that has simply become good at integrating everything. Sanity then fails in three ways: through rigid contraction around a single level of abstraction, through too much flexibility with no external feedback that only provides resonance, or through apathy that makes nothing worth targeting.

Without the average adult-filtered gestalt to compress input into, children reach for whatever priors they do have, often animate and relational, and build a representation out of them.

Take this monologue from a child in this short-form video:

> Why do humans skip rocks?
>
> I think that the river likes games. Not big games, just tiny ones.
>
> You throw a bad rock, it eats it. You throw a good one, it lets it skip a few times… like it’s decising like no, noo, maybe, no, yes!
>
> You think you’re throwing rocks but really you’re asking the river a question and every time it answer.

Adults have the compressed and factually correct dead gestalts that explain rock skipping like angles and spin. The child’s version does not correspond with reality, but it is genuinely “alive” and an act of construction reaching across a large fit-distance to build a vivid gestalt, mapping the felt sense of the event onto the embodied, animate priors they have stored. This is the same metaphor mechanism described earlier in the essay, but it is coming from a less-pruned, less conditioned field so it takes a different orientation.

"I think the optimal state of maturity for an adult is to be like a Wise Old Child" [Vacha states on Twitter](https://x.com/TVachaW/status/2012933004562202956?a=K0etcOt8FcWfLYsdbkk\&s=20). This is the mature form of childlike wonder. A child sees things as transparent without trying, because their field has not yet solidified into reifications, which is why so many things feel fresh, vivid and new. The average adult's world feels solid and dull because heavy conditioning has compressed things into stable, cheap to integrate, reified chunks. Interestingly, psychedelics have similar results through loosening rigid deep priors, just as seeing emptiness and releasing contraction does. As Michael Pollan puts it, "My own gut is there is something childlike about the consciousness at certain stages in the trip. Just judging from the experience of having had a kid, that there are levels of sensitivity to environment that children have that we lose as adults because of our desire to focus. We shut things out." Contemplative practice is partly a deliberate return to that newness, not by having fewer priors like the child, but by holding them in expansion rather than contraction: keeping them as light as a feather but as useful as a sharp knife, seeing their emptiness while keeping the competence.

This is not simply regression to childhood. The Wise Old Child's openness comes from having many ontologies and frames, and holding them as objects rather than being swept away by them. They are seen as transparent. Robert Kegan in his 5-stage model of his constructive-developmental theory describes this, where what we were once subject to becomes an object we can hold: “[All evolution of consciousness as we understand it always involves moving what was subject to an object](https://webmail.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/20/05/covid-crisis-and-adult-development).” David Chapman describes this way of relating to meaning as “fluidity,” which he associates with the fifth stage of adult development. [Fluidity is the ability to notice and work within different systems of meaning without being bound by any singular one](https://meaningness.com/cofounders-in-relationship/comments#:~:text=In%20the%20fluid%20mode%2C%20you%20don%E2%80%99t%20operate%20according%20to%20systems%3B%20you%20operate%20taking%20systems%20into%20account%2C%20but%20are%20not%20bound%20by%20them.).&#x20;

We may describe children as whimsical and full of life, but not wise. So what are they missing? Orientation towards targets that serve well-refined purposes.

A conman that scams people can be sane; they can read a situation well, move between states flexibly, and update when they need to. Their system is *sane* as they revise gestalts when they are met with error which moves them towards their held targets. But, we would not consider this wise. Flexibility alone does not choose the right targets we should be oriented towards.

What makes this hard to fully state is that wisdom does not seem to be algorithmic. Wisdom seems closer to something known through negation, like good health, which is not something added on top of experience, but what remains once contraction preventing flexibility is released. Here, you do not acquire wisdom by just adopting the correct model, you understand it by releasing the contraction through safety, and reified structure that were blurring what you could see and do when in other states. Wittgenstein's point applies here too, since the target(s) of wisdom cannot be fully compressed into a statement of what it is and what it is aiming towards. This makes negation easier to describe wisdom because it is side-stepping the direct compression needed to put its felt sense into words. This is because holding gestalts as transparent that we must use for structure is a difficult task due to an apparent “invisible” quality. It might be said wisdom is reflexive, only known after-the-fact through examination of the person-environment system, but distortion is still needed to be dropped for it to be cultivated and used in the first place. However, just because some contraction is dropped does not mean wisdom is guaranteed. This is the case of the spiritual leader who has ethical scandals or acts in such a way that it is obvious some amount of blockage is still present in their field preventing their dependency tree from containing what  .

Removing targets from our bow tie(s) or field is impossible and not the goal of wisdom. From the suffering loop, direction comes from the target while demand comes from the second-order control we believe we must enact to prevent something intolerable. Wisdom here has the ability to keep the direction but drop the pressure, which makes action decisive without a single action policy feeling solid or final. Equanimity here comes into play as a side effect by leaving targets and actions available without making them feel necessary in experience. Apathy contrasts this by dropping salience across the field so nothing feels worth targeting. Wisdom and equanimity work together by leaving more options alive for the mind-body system to enact well-calibrated policies by dropping pressure.

One pragmatic example of this is holding a cup: useful tension is used to clearly execute the target of drinking out of the cup, and the tension is dropped when it is not needed. Of course, "not needed" is a privileged perspective, meaning, it is not needed because the target is simply "drink water." Ulimtelty we cannot solely blame tension for our suffering as there are targets ensuring this tension stays alive. It can be said that anywhere tension or suffering is, it is playing a practical purpose in our system. We may not like that purpose and the "reasoning" may be outdated, but it is true there are incentive structures in the system carrying the top-down targets needed so this suffering stays alive as the most salient option to the system. Of course, we can have competing interpretations and prediction trees that seemingly clash, but ultimately the system must decide on one thing, and anytime that one thing is chosen, it is serving a purpose. Going back to the cup example to drink water, the tension there is useful and dropped when needed. This in the same way is like holding mental models in tension and dropping them when needed, like when updating our knowledge about how something works. We can even back this up to our framing of things themselves: from which ways can we hold epistemic patterns in place using tension which serves our targets, while easily dropping what does not, including useful tension? One might call this "[frame wizardry](https://www.octopusyarn.com/p/wisdom-is-frame-wizardry-12)": the ability to change the ontologies you are working in to think more clearly and handle situations better. This becomes a sort of "fighting move" of constantly dropping and reinstantiating useful and useless contraction. Understanding this process becomes a skill that ubiquitously effects your life through increasing well-being and intelligence. It is the meta-skill of all skills.

In terms of the bow tie architecture, this becomes a problem of compression and generative freedom. Each new frame or way of looking becomes a way of compressing experience differently, preserving some relations while leaving others out. Holding an ontology as an object lets us notice what it makes more obvious and what it constrains, while finding useful invariant gestalts that carry across frames. We still commit to interpretations and actions, but neither becomes the only possible way of organizing the situation, as even what we preserve remains mutable.&#x20;

If we take a look back at section 2.4 on language, even actors are doing this all the time. Actors must intentionally take the stance of a character's perspective and emotions, which naturally changes how they speak and behave. This downstream speech and behavior is a natural result of the tension being held, just like the tension we carry when holding a cup to drink water from it. And just as we seamlessly drop the tension after drinking the cup, actors drop the tension once they are done playing their character. In both instances, we can wisely recognize when contraction is or is not serving us, dropping it, and reinstating a new stance that brings us closer to reaching our targets. In both instances, constraints are set in the person's bow tie(s), such as act this way or don't do this, and what falls out of that is naturally generated behavior.

Practically, a useful model here is running the loop of refinement with transparency underlying its process. When a gestalt forms, you can recognize it as a construction and which level of abstraction it sits at like being too detailed or too broad. You can attempt to check its ontological authority what alternatives that is preventing you from seeing. And you can question what in the field needs revising like precision, the model itself, the targets, or the action policies. Then all of it can run again, iterating on itself. Crucially, there is no global endpoint here, there never is to insight, both in terms of wisdom and intellectual capacity. This is because holding a structure as transparent eventually ends up as the default in the field, which then eventually becomes transparent in its own turn. Each structure you see through becomes available to be objectified for the next round, so each iteration of refinement updates what is reachable in the field by expanding the space of what is available as interpretation and action rather than shrinking availabilities. The priors underlying all of this are not thrown away, just reinstantiated moment-to-moment. This is why holding different levels of the dependency tree as transparent matters, as it enables you to change your relationship to the structure rather than just sitting on the same insights.

Take an instance of a friend not replying to your texts. A default thought pattern of "they don't care about me forms," and eventually you notice this one instance triggers a cascade of judgements, which are an attempt to find pattern in ambiguity. In that pattern, deep priors are hit with prediction error and high precision is set on those priors such as judgements dealing with your self worth and how you relate to others. Eventually you might consider other explanations of what happened and lower your confidence in your own judgement by seeing its transparency. Of course none of this is separate from the body too: you might recognize how internal feelings are being bound as one gestalt which is organizing and perpetuating this pattern. Through seeing transparency, our binding techniques start to play less of a role in this pattern and we can discern between what is "really" going on. With practice, this type of awareness and questioning becomes automatic, eventually shifting to deeper constraints in the bow tie, like what is upstream of that painful gestalt.&#x20;

All of this is driven by a recognition that felt senses can be wrong, both friction and resonance. Sanity and wisdom is not about arrive at a singular, correct representation or target. It is understanding that flexibility and feedback are crucial to increase well-being and our intellectual capacity, and knowing that maps are never the territory, but they can still be reliable guides for useful action.
