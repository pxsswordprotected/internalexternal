First, a definition: a haptic contraction is a felt tightening or clenching in bodily sensation, whether muscular, visceral, or vascular. It is the somatic form a held prior takes in direct experience, whether it is being violated or simply held for use. Its counterpart, haptic expansion, is simply the release of this kind of contraction. In this sense, contraction refers more specifically to tension that participates in maintaining or responding to an active field configuration.

In the terms of the previous section, haptic contraction is one way the body holds a target: it keeps a state constant until it becomes true. This is why target errors persist while ordinary prediction error fades.

Not all contraction is negative in its relation to well-being. Functional contraction holds a configuration in place for action, like a skill or an idea held in mind, and can be released when the task ends, modulated by targets.

One possible mechanistic low-level account here is Michael Edward Johnson's "vasocomputation" hypothesis, which proposes that active-inference predictions may be physically instantiated through vascular tension. In this account, a contraction first collapses an ambiguous neural pattern into a more definite one, holds it as a form of memory, and, if sustained, latches into a durable pattern that resists updating until the prediction resolves or the latch is released.

These haptic contractions may match the depth of the priors being held, meaning that deeper priors produce stronger, more persistent, and "physically deeper" contractions. I predict this can potentially be seen physiologically within the vasocomputation framework. It is known **that** vascular tone has a very large dynamic range as local muscle blood flow can vary \~20-50x depending on the state you are in, such as coming off of exercise (measured in skeletal muscle during exercise, which shows the range of vascular tone, not anything about priors). It might be that priors that are deeply held, which might maintain themselves as distributed networks of latches that are more "sticky" through smooth muscle contraction and stay alive for years, would restrict local muscle blood flow at a greater level than specific branch-level priors that can be swapped moment-to-moment. Teleologically, if networks of deep priors did allow for more local blood flow, these associative latches would be more inclined to expand and update, which plays against our own energetic needs as **updating these frequently** would be costly and not suitable for survival as an organism. Chronic contraction can still have its own costs like collapsing possibilities, but **it** is likely still less costly than updating load-bearing priors.

Some tension can be released within seconds because it is associated with priors on outer branches of the dependency tree, while other tension remains stable across many situations because it is associated with deeper, more foundational priors. Michael Johnson **differentiates** this through latched and unlatched tension: tension that releases in seconds is unlatched, and tension that stays stable across situations is latched. Releasing an old latch does not automatically restore the patterns it closed off (see section 6.2).

Taken together, these three accounts describe the same process from different sides: the dependency tree gives the structure its depth, the Energetic Triad explains the magnitude of the resulting spikes, and haptic contraction provides a physical substrate through which the structure is stabilized and enacted. Depth in the tree predicts how costly a prior is to update and how firmly its associated tension is latched. This means the meditation/contemplative work of releasing contraction and the epistemic work of updating knowledge may be closely related mechanistically, since both loosen the precision and embodied reinforcement that keep a deep prior stable, making the underlying model more available for updating.

Deeply conditioned priors can be expressed and stabilized through recurring haptic contraction patterns so they can be enacted. These patterns do not instantiate only single priors, but instead help instantiate whole field configurations, each of which may come with a characteristic pattern of haptic contraction and release that supports its properties like targets and constraints. These patterns may also keep the targets associated with those priors active after the original situation has passed so they can be resolved. By holding unresolved prediction or target error in the embodied layer, they keep attention and action weighted toward signals that might reduce the discrepancy or satisfy the target. Active priors can generate contraction, while the resulting interoceptive signals provide new evidence that the predicted state is real and meaningful. Episodic recall here is doing something similar in which reinstating the configuration dealing with the memory, the internal representations, reinstates its somatic pattern, since this is how the configuration is enacted.

If we take a look at a mathematician in the middle of solving a problem, their tacit knowledge, embodied through conditioned patterns of haptic contraction, is activated by the current problem and target to form a specific field configuration that comes with its own unique properties. Each new representation, such as a mental image, is then tested against the embodied felt sense of that field, with a negative difference in felt tone or friction signaling that it does not fit and a positive difference or resonance signaling fit.

Again, I turn to the vasocomputation hypothesis as well as this tweet by Michael Johnson himself that proposes something very similar, which I did not see until after I put together the above paragraph. He describes these recurring patterns of muscular and vascular tension as "stances," which closely parallels the claim here that field configurations have characteristic somatic patterns that stabilize predictions and make certain actions available.

As stated earlier, single releases of contraction usually affect outer branches of the dependency tree, but repeated releases can gradually propagate toward the trunk as the field learns that the contraction is no longer necessary. With greater sensory clarity and regulatory slack, meditation allows the system to remain with prediction error without immediately restoring the old contraction through active inference. Over time, this can reduce the precision and embodied reinforcement of deeper priors, making increasingly load-bearing patterns available for updating. This is the same general process through which tacit knowledge develops, but applied in the opposite direction: repeated experience can both embody useful structures and loosen embodied structures that no longer serve their original function. Indeed, this mechanism helps explain why meditation can occasionally produce psychedelic-like moments through the release of deep tension, as psychedelics similarly loosen these rigid, deep priors.

Connecting this to the representational layer, all representations bring their own properties into our field and reweight active priors. A representation imports properties into the field, and the body enacts them via active inference, so the somatic change is downstream of the field re-tuning, not the representation mysteriously touching muscle. It's important to remember that these changes that the representation brings to the field are typically more surface level, affecting prediction branches further out, so the somatic changes it produces are usually the fast, unlatched kind (you tense or relax in the moment, and it releases when the representation drops), whereas the deep latched contractions that come from deep priors don't change just because you adopted a new representation. For example, a representation of safety won't do much if the body remains braced since interoceptive evidence still signals danger. Now, nuance here is needed since there are instances in which representations, over time, can indeed affect the system deeply, like the practice of the Ideal Parent Figure protocol developed by Dr. Daniel P. Brown and David Elliott. In this instance, repeated imagery of attuned caregivers gives the field a stable representation of secure attachment that can be reinstantiated across sessions. In the terms of this framework, repeatedly using that representation while it produces an embodied sense of safety may gradually reduce the precision of older attachment predictions and train the new pattern(s) into the embodied layer, allowing an initially temporary representation to influence deeper priors and their coupled contractions. This results in a new field configuration and destabilizes the old attractor state and its basin, allowing for more flexibility. Importantly, time and repetition alone are not sufficient for a representation to reach deeper priors. The representation must generate trusted evidence and support actions that repeatedly produce outcomes the system can use to override its older predictions.

Then moving to the notational layer, we must first remember **that** anything in this layer requires compression of at least the embodied layer. Insight originates as an embodied felt sense and gets compressed into representation or notation, such as Einstein's "muscular type" thoughts: "the above-mentioned elements are, in my case, of visual and some of muscular type." Now, since the embodied layer is so involved with this process of bringing forth notation, and we have established that priors are physically held as tension/contraction, then the physical intuition behind these insights is the body's stored prior structure coming alive as a felt sense. So embodiment-in-the-layer-sense and embodiment-in-the-somatic-sense overlap heavily and are best understood as the same process. We can come to an implied conclusion that pure symbol interaction with no grounded intuition rarely produces genuine insight on its own. Because notation is always a lossy translation of the embodied, with no felt sense driving the interaction, there's nothing being compressed and no positive difference in felt tone guiding which interactions are meaningful. We've all felt this before: we are reading something, and five minutes later we realize we have **retained almost nothing** because no loop is running to get feedback from our embodied layer; it's just pure symbol input. This does not mean notational manipulation is useless without the embodied layer, since notation can function as external scaffolding and generate structures that can become embodied later. It's that notation becomes meaningful to the system through feedback with the representational and embodied layers rather than through notation alone.

Just as an example, we can take the names "OpenAI" vs "ClosedAI." Both these words instantiate new field configurations, like open being expansive and safe and closed being contracted and guarded, along with all the implications associated with each that propagate through the system. The priors associated with "open" and "closed" are activated "automatically" here, since the notation is so common that its implications have been trained over lots of repetition. Because of embodiment, a representation that imports branches associated with "closed" produces actual haptic contraction, and one that imports branches associated with "open" produces a sense of release, even if very subtly. Keep in mind all of this is related to the individual and dependent on context and conditioning. "Open" can imply freedom in one's conditioning and vulnerability in another, while "closed" can imply restriction or protection depending on the active target. All of this is a small case of a broader point developed in 2.4: certain wording indexes a pattern of tension, which is why framing changes perception.
